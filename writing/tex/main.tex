\title{Stiffening, turning and snapping: how the membrane-tension response of protein elastic networks departs from linearization}
\author[a]{Jian Wang\corref{cor1}}
\ead{wang_j@zju.edu.cn}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author.}
\address[a]{[AFFILIATION TO CONFIRM: department, institution, city, postal code, country]}

\begin{abstract}
Anisotropic network models of proteins are the reference-state linearization of a geometrically nonlinear network of Hookean springs. We compute the nonlinear equilibrium response of C$\alpha$ truss models to a conservative membrane tension for six mechanosensitive channels with tension-related partner structures and two control structures. It departs from the linearization in three ways. The network stiffens in \V{n-stiff} structures, so that the linear response overshoots, by a factor of \V{amp-piezo10} for Piezo1 at $\eps=0.10$; a single amplitude removes this. The response turns: the direction error that no rescaling removes is ranked across structures by a second-order perturbation indicator from the reference state (Spearman $\rho_s=$\V{chi-rank}). And the stable branch can end: at the reference cutoff only Piezo1 reaches a limit point, localized in one subunit at element strains beyond a backbone criterion, and none of the other \V{n-stable} has a negative eigenvalue on its computed path. The nonlinear analysis thus adds a verified path with an inertia-count stability check and a validity range. Against the partner structures only Piezo1, the channel that turns most, comes closer along the nonlinear path than along any rescaled linearization (transmembrane C$\alpha$ RMSD \V{piezo-nl} vs \V{piezo-lin}\,\AA), but an isotropic in-plane dilation (\V{piezo-dil}\,\AA) and rewired networks reach the same agreement. The path uses a gauge that follows the load symmetries (isostatic anchors turn the tilting moment of a projected-area tension into distorting point reactions) and is reproduced by an explicit solver to $\le$\V{vf-max}\,\AA.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
%======================================================================
Elastic network models represent a folded protein as nodes, usually C$\alpha$ atoms, joined by Hookean springs between all pairs closer than a cutoff \citep{tirion1996,atilgan2001,eyal2006,yang2009}. Their normal modes reproduce crystallographic B-factors and frequently the direction of functional transitions \citep{togashi2018,valadie2003}. The anisotropic network model (ANM) is not a linear model of a linear system: it is the linearization, at the reference configuration, of a network whose element lengths are nonlinear functions of the nodal positions. The tangent stiffness of that network contains a stress (geometric) part that vanishes only at the stress-free reference. A large conformational change therefore raises a question of structural mechanics: how, and how much, does the finite-deformation response of the \emph{same} network depart from its linearization?

Along a load path, a geometrically nonlinear structure can depart from its linearization in three ways \citep{thompson1973}. It can \emph{stiffen} (or soften), so that the response grows more slowly (or faster) than linearly with the load. Its response can \emph{turn}, so that the deformation pattern itself changes along the path. And it can \emph{snap}, when the stable branch ends at a limit point or bifurcation. For an elastic network that is compared with experiment through a structural displacement, the three departures have different consequences. Stiffening changes only the amplitude, which ANM users choose anyway. Turning changes the predicted pattern and is the only departure that a rescaled linear response cannot reproduce. Snapping ends the regime in which a smooth equilibrium path exists. This paper asks which of the three occur when protein networks are loaded by membrane tension, whether they can be anticipated from the reference state, and which of them matter for agreement with experiment.

Linear response theory \citep{ikeguchi2005} and perturbation-response scanning \citep{atilgan2009} use the reference-state linearization to predict the response to a force; the adaptive ANM \citep{yang2009aanm} updates the network along a transition between two known end states. Relaxation in nonlinear elastic networks \citep{togashi2007}, multi-basin and plastic network models \citep{miyashita2003,maragakis2005,chu2007} and elastic-network interpolation \citep{kim2002} change the energy function or prescribe the end state. Finite-element formulations of protein mechanics \citep{bathe2008} and continuum models of mechanosensitive (MS) channels resolve the channel and the membrane as elastic bodies and compute gating under tension \citep{wiggins2004,tang2006}; coarse-grained molecular simulations have followed MscL under tension \citep{yefimov2008,louhivuori2010}, and coarse-grained Piezo1 studies have prescribed the deformation \citep{zheng2017}. None of these separates the departures of an otherwise unchanged network from its linearization under a load that is itself a membrane tension. MS channels are a natural test bed. They open in response to membrane tension \citep{perozo2003,sukharev2001,chiang2004}, their gating transitions are large (the Piezo1 dome flattens and expands \citep{guo2017,saotome2018,lin2019,yang2022,mulhall2023,liu2025,haselwandter2018}, the MscS transmembrane helices tilt \citep{wang2008,reddy2019}, and the K2P channels move TM4 between down and up states \citep{brohawn2014,dong2015}), and several of them have a tension-related partner structure, so a computed response can be compared with experiment without fitting.

Measuring the three departures requires a path that is well defined and trustworthy. The load must be a membrane tension, not a prescribed displacement. The removal of rigid-body motion must not inject reactions, which, as we show, requires attention to which rigid motions the tension potential is invariant under. The path must be verified independently of the solver, and the sign of the lowest eigenvalue of its tangent must be established at every step by an inertia count rather than inferred from a few eigenvalues, because snapping is one of the quantities to be measured.

Section~\ref{sec:form} formulates the conservative tension load, derives the admissible gauges from its symmetries, and defines one measure for each departure: the amplitude ratio for stiffening, the direction error and its second-order indicator for turning, and the inertia of the constrained tangent for snapping. Section~\ref{sec:num} gives the solvers and the stability check, and Section~\ref{sec:val} the partner structures and null models. Section~\ref{sec:res} establishes the path; Sections~\ref{sec:stiff}, \ref{sec:turn} and \ref{sec:snap} report stiffening, turning and snapping in turn. In brief, all networks stiffen, which a single amplitude removes; a second-order indicator ranks the turning across structures; in the one channel whose nonlinear path is closer to its partner than the rescaled linearization, Piezo1, the gain equals that of an isotropic dilation; and at the reference cutoff only Piezo1 loses stability, at a localized limit point.

\section{Formulation}
\label{sec:form}
%======================================================================
\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Geometrically nonlinear C$\alpha$ truss}{Geometrically nonlinear C-alpha truss}}
Let $\x\in\mathbb{R}^{3N}$ collect the positions of the $N$ C$\alpha$ nodes and $\x_0$ their reference (closed-state) positions. Every node pair with $\|\x_{0,j}-\x_{0,i}\|<r_c$ is joined by a two-node truss element $e$ with rest length $\ell_e^0$ and current length $\ell_e(\x)$. With $k_e$ the element stiffness, the stored energy and the tangent stiffness are
\begin{equation}
U(\x)=\tfrac12\sum_e k_e\bigl(\ell_e-\ell_e^0\bigr)^2,\qquad
\Kt(\x)=\underbrace{\sum_e \mathbf{G}_e\otimes k_e\,\mathbf{n}_e\mathbf{n}_e^{T}}_{\mathbf{K}_m}
+\underbrace{\sum_e \mathbf{G}_e\otimes \frac{T_e}{\ell_e}\bigl(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{n}_e\mathbf{n}_e^{T}\bigr)}_{\mathbf{K}_\sigma},
\label{eq:U}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{n}_e$ is the current element direction, $T_e=k_e(\ell_e-\ell_e^0)$ the axial force and $\mathbf{G}_e$ the $N\times N$ incidence pattern of the element ($+1$ on the two diagonal entries, $-1$ off-diagonal), so that each element contributes a $2\times2$ block pattern of $3\times3$ blocks. $\mathbf{K}_m$ is the material stiffness, which depends on the configuration through the rotation of $\mathbf{n}_e$, and $\mathbf{K}_\sigma$ the stress stiffness. The kinematics is exact and the constitutive law is linear, so all nonlinearity is geometric. At $\x=\x_0$ every $T_e$ vanishes and $\Kt(\x_0)$ is the ANM Hessian. Element strains are denoted $\eta_e=\ell_e/\ell_e^0-1$. We use $r_c=10$\,\AA{} as the reference cutoff, and repeat every calculation at $r_c=8$ and $12$\,\AA. A backbone variant multiplies $k_e$ of the virtual bonds between consecutive residues by $10$ or $100$ (Section~\ref{sec:robust}).

\emph{Units.} Lengths are in \AA{} and all elements share one stiffness $k$ (force per length). We report the dimensionless tension $\hat\tau=\tau/k$, both $\tau$ and $k$ being forces per length, energies in units of $k$\,\AA$^2$ and eigenvalues of the tangent in units of $k$; the linear compliance $\eps_\tau$ introduced below is then in units of $1/k$. We drop the hat; Section~\ref{sec:phys} converts $\tau$ to a physical tension.

\subsection{Conservative membrane tension}
\label{sec:load}
Each structure is taken in the membrane orientation of the OPM database \citep{lomize2006}, with $z$ along the membrane normal and the bilayer midplane at $z_m$. Membrane nodes are the C$\alpha$ with $|z-z_m|\le h$, where $h$ is the OPM hydrophobic half-thickness ($h=$\V{ht-min}--\V{ht-max}\,\AA; for TRAAK the default \V{ht-traak}\,\AA{} was used instead of the OPM value \V{ht-traak-opm}\,\AA). The slab is divided into 4\,\AA{} layers and each layer into $n_s$ angular sectors ($n_s\approx2\pi R/6$\,\AA, so that $n_s=$\V{nsec-min}--\V{nsec-max} across the panel). In each sector the \emph{outer shell} is the set of membrane nodes within 4\,\AA{} of the sector's outermost radius, and the polygon vertex is the mean in-plane position of the shell. The vertices are fixed linear combinations of nodal coordinates, $\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{P}\x$, so the shoelace area of each layer polygon, averaged over layers, is an exact quadratic form,
\begin{equation}
A(\x)=\tfrac12\,\x^{T}\Hm\,\x,\qquad \Hm=\mathbf{P}^{T}\Hm_p\mathbf{P},\qquad
\Pi(\x;\tau)=U(\x)-\tau A(\x),
\label{eq:A}
\end{equation}
where $\Hm_p$ is the constant, layer-averaged shoelace matrix acting on the in-plane vertex coordinates, $A=\tfrac12\mathbf{v}^T\Hm_p\mathbf{v}$. The load $\mathbf{f}=\tau\Hm\x$ derives from a potential, the problem is conservative and the tangent $\Kt-\tau\Hm$ is symmetric; $-\tau\Hm$ is the load stiffness. Averaging over the shell spreads the tension over \V{nload-min}--\V{nload-max} nodes per channel (Fig.~\ref{fig:model}b). No displacement is prescribed: the pore and the gate are free. The path is parametrized by the normalized radial displacement of the membrane nodes, $\eps=\langle(\x-\x_0)\cdot\hat{\mathbf{e}}_r\rangle/\langle r_0\rangle$, a response quantity and not a strain.

\subsection{Symmetries of the load and the gauge}
\label{sec:gauge}
Equilibrium $\nabla\Pi=\mathbf{0}$ is defined up to the rigid motions under which $\Pi$ is invariant. $U$ is invariant under all six. $A$ depends only on in-plane vertex coordinates, so it is invariant, for every $\x$, under the three translations and under rotation about the membrane normal; by Noether's argument the load has zero resultant force and zero moment $M_z$ in every configuration. $A$ is \emph{not} invariant under the two tilts: tilting a stack of layers changes the projected polygon areas, and since the vertices are shell means of nodes spread over a 4\,\AA{} layer, the discrete reference orientation is not exactly a stationary point of $A$. The load therefore carries a small tilting moment $M_{xy}$, of relative size \V{mxy-min}--\V{mxy-max} at $\x_0$ (Table~\ref{tab:ver}). This moment belongs to the model load, not to the solver: a projected-area tension resists tilt, with stiffness $\sim\tau A$.

This dictates the gauge, by which we mean the constraints that remove rigid motion in the numerical solution. Constraining only the four exact symmetries, $\C_4(\x-\x_0)=\mathbf{0}$, gives a constraint reaction that vanishes identically and leaves the tilts free, stabilized by $-\tau\Hm$. The six Eckart conditions \citep{eckart1935}, $\C(\x-\x_0)=\mathbf{0}$ with multipliers $\bm\mu$, additionally suppress tilt; their reaction $\C^{T}\bm\mu$ is the tilting moment distributed as a rigid-rotation field. Six isostatic nodal anchors suppress the same tilt with point reactions whose magnitude is the moment divided by the anchor lever arm. We use the six Eckart conditions in production as a numerical gauge, because they keep the constrained tangent regular at $\tau\to0$ (the tilt stiffness vanishes with $\tau$), and quantify their effect against the four-symmetry gauge for every channel (Section~\ref{sec:wellposed}). In bordered form, a Newton increment solves
\begin{equation}
\begin{bmatrix}\Kt-\tau\Hm & \C^{T}\\ \C & \mathbf{0}\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}\Delta\x\\ \bm\mu\end{bmatrix}
=\begin{bmatrix}-\nabla\Pi\\ -\C(\x-\x_0)\end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:bordered}
\end{equation}
We report the reaction ratio $q=\|\C^T\bm\mu\|/\|\tau\Hm\x\|$ and the largest rigid rotation between $\x$ and $\x_0$, which stays below \V{rot-max}$^\circ$ on all paths, so that constraints written in the reference orientation remain an adequate definition of the rigid motion. An alternative that makes the load orthogonal to all six reference rigid modes, $A'(\x)=A(\mathbf{S}\x)$ with the oblique projector $\mathbf{S}=\mathbf{I}-\bm\Phi(\bm\Phi_L^T\bm\Phi)^{-1}\bm\Phi_L^T$, where $\bm\Phi$ is the $3N\times6$ rigid-body basis of $\x_0$ and $\bm\Phi_L$ its restriction to the loaded nodes, was tested and rejected: it removes $M_{xy}$ at $\x_0$ but increases the imbalance in the deformed configuration (Section~\ref{sec:wellposed}).

\subsection{Three measures of departure from linearization}
\label{sec:enl}
\label{sec:chi}
\emph{Stiffening and turning.} With $\mathbf{u}_{NL}$ the nonlinear displacement and $\mathbf{u}_L$ the linear one at the same tension, both with rigid motion removed, let $a=\|\mathbf{u}_L\|/\|\mathbf{u}_{NL}\|$ be the amplitude ratio and $\theta$ the angle between them. The linearization error then splits exactly into
\begin{equation}
E_{NL}^2=\frac{\|\mathbf{u}_{NL}-\mathbf{u}_L\|^2}{\|\mathbf{u}_{NL}\|^2}=(a-\cos\theta)^2+\sin^2\theta,
\qquad \min_s\frac{\|\mathbf{u}_{NL}-s\,\mathbf{u}_L\|}{\|\mathbf{u}_{NL}\|}=\sin\theta .
\label{eq:enl}
\end{equation}
The amplitude ratio measures stiffening ($a>1$) or softening ($a<1$); the direction error $\sin\theta$ measures turning. Choosing the amplitude of the linear response, as every ANM comparison does, removes the amplitude part exactly and leaves $\sin\theta$. We evaluate both on the membrane nodes at the common load levels $\eps=0.05$ and $0.10$ rather than at channel-specific end points: at the step of the nonlinear path where its own $\eps$ reaches that value, with $\mathbf{u}_L$ taken at the same tension.

\emph{Second-order indicators.} Expanding the constrained equilibrium as $\x=\x_0+\tau\mathbf{u}_1+\tau^2\mathbf{u}_2+O(\tau^3)$ gives
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{K}_0\mathbf{u}_1=\Hm\x_0,\qquad
\mathbf{K}_0\mathbf{u}_2=\Hm\mathbf{u}_1-\tfrac12\,D^3U(\x_0)[\mathbf{u}_1,\mathbf{u}_1],
\label{eq:pert}
\end{equation}
both Eckart-constrained, with $\mathbf{K}_0=\Kt(\x_0)$; the third-derivative term is evaluated by a central second difference of $\nabla U$ along $\mathbf{u}_1$. On the right of the second equation, $\Hm\mathbf{u}_1$ is the change of the load with the configuration, and $D^3U(\x_0)[\mathbf{u}_1,\cdot]$ is the linear change of the tangent stiffness along $\mathbf{u}_1$, which contains both the rotation of the element directions in $\mathbf{K}_m$ and the stress stiffness $\mathbf{K}_\sigma$ that the stretched elements acquire. To leading order the amplitude change is $\kappa=\tau\,\mathbf{u}_2\cdot\mathbf{u}_1/\|\mathbf{u}_1\|^2$, so that $a\approx1/(1+\kappa)$ and $\kappa<0$ indicates stiffening, and the direction error is $\sin\theta\approx\chi=\tau\|\mathbf{u}_2^{\perp}\|/\|\mathbf{u}_1\|$, with $\mathbf{u}_2^{\perp}$ the part of $\mathbf{u}_2$ orthogonal to $\mathbf{u}_1$. Both use only the reference state. We evaluate them on the membrane nodes at $\tau_{\mathrm{ref}}=\eps/\eps_\tau$, the tension at which the linear response reaches the same $\eps$, with $\eps_\tau$ the linear response $\eps$ per unit tension. The measured and the predicted quantities therefore refer to different tensions at the same $\eps$: because the networks stiffen, the nonlinear path reaches a given $\eps$ at a tension above $\tau_{\mathrm{ref}}$. The comparison tests rank and order of magnitude, not a pointwise identity.

\emph{Snapping.} The equilibrium is stable when the tangent $\Kt-\tau\Hm$ is positive definite on the constrained space. Its lowest eigenvalue $\lambda_1$ rises along the path when the network stiffens; a limit point or bifurcation ends the stable branch where an eigenvalue reaches zero, which need not be the eigenvalue that was lowest at the reference. We establish the sign of the lowest eigenvalue by counting negative eigenvalues (Section~\ref{sec:inertia}).

\section{Numerical methods}
\label{sec:num}
%======================================================================
\subsection{Equilibrium path}
\emph{Newton.} Equation~\eqref{eq:bordered} is solved with backtracking on $\Pi$; a load step is accepted at a relative residual of $10^{-9}$, the increment grows by 25\% after easy steps and is halved on failure; for Piezo1 it is capped at $\Delta\eps\approx0.001$ beyond $\eps=0.08$. The bordered system is solved with a sparse symmetric-indefinite $\mathbf{L}\mathbf{D}\mathbf{L}^T$ factorization of the bordered matrix, the same factorization that gives the inertia (Section~\ref{sec:inertia}), with two steps of iterative refinement; its sparsity pattern is fixed per network, so the symbolic analysis is done once. A Newton iteration is abandoned as failed, and the increment halved, when three consecutive line searches reach the smallest step length.

\emph{VFIFE.} The vector-form intrinsic finite-element method \citep{ting2004,yao2011,wang2021,tan2021} follows nodal motion by explicit central differences and evaluates element forces in a convected frame after removing the element's rigid motion by reverse rotation. For a two-node truss the deformation-coordinate force equals the total-Lagrangian force exactly, so VFIFE solves the same discrete problem by a different algorithm: an explicit, damped dynamic relaxation \citep{ramesh1993,lee2011,alamatian2013,rezaiee2011}. Each node carries a fictitious isotropic mass equal to half the largest Gershgorin row sum of its $3\times3$ block row of the tangent, which keeps the unit time step below the critical step; the damping coefficient is $2\omega_{\min}$, with $\omega_{\min}$ the lowest natural frequency of the constrained tangent with these masses; and the iteration stops when the out-of-balance force and the kinetic energy fall below $10^{-8}$ and $10^{-13}$. With $\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{f}}$ the diagonal matrix of fictitious masses, the Eckart conditions enter as mass-weighted constraint forces,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{f}_c=\mathbf{f}+\C^{T}\bm\mu,\qquad
\bm\mu=-(\C\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{f}}^{-1}\C^{T})^{-1}\C\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{f}}^{-1}\mathbf{f},
\end{equation}
so that $\C\ddot{\x}=\C\dot{\x}=\C(\x-\x_0)=\mathbf{0}$ hold exactly and the rest state satisfies the constrained equilibrium of Eq.~\eqref{eq:bordered}. VFIFE is restarted at rest from converged Newton states and driven to the next tension, so that the cross-check covers increments that end at the validity boundary. It is also used to compute the snap at the Piezo1 limit point (Section~\ref{sec:snap}).

\emph{Arclength continuation.} Beyond the last converged load-controlled step the path is followed by Keller's pseudo-arclength method \citep{keller1977,riks1979,crisfield1981} in the variables $(\x,\bm\mu,\Lambda)$, with the load parameter $\Lambda=\tau\,\eps_\tau$. Each corrector iteration requires two solves with the bordered tangent. A step is accepted only if the corrector converges in at most 12 iterations and successive unit tangents satisfy $\mathbf{t}_k\cdot\mathbf{t}_{k+1}\ge0.98$; otherwise the arclength increment is halved, so that folds are resolved rather than jumped.

\subsection{Stability by inertia count}
\label{sec:inertia}
The equilibrium is stable when $\Kt-\tau\Hm$ is positive definite on the constrained space $\{\Delta\x:\C\Delta\x=\mathbf{0}\}$. For a bordered matrix with $\C$ of full row rank $m$, $\mathrm{In}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{K}&\C^T\\\C&\mathbf{0}\end{bmatrix}=\mathrm{In}(\mathbf{Z}^T\mathbf{K}\mathbf{Z})+(m,m,0)$, with $\mathbf{Z}$ a null-space basis of $\C$. The constrained tangent is therefore positive definite if and only if the bordered matrix has exactly $m$ negative and no zero eigenvalues. We obtain the inertia at every accepted step from a sparse symmetric-indefinite $\mathbf{L}\mathbf{D}\mathbf{L}^T$ factorization of the bordered matrix with Bunch--Kaufman-type $1\times1$ and $2\times2$ pivoting \citep{bunch1977} (MKL PARDISO; a dense Bunch--Kaufman factorization is used instead whenever a pivot had to be perturbed), which counts \emph{all} negative eigenvalues. Zero eigenvalues are not read from pivots but counted by a shift count, $n_0=n_-(\mathbf{K}-s\mathbf{I})-n_-(\mathbf{K}+s\mathbf{I})$ with $s=10^{-8}$ in model units, where $n_-$ is the number of negative eigenvalues of the constrained matrix; $n_{\mathrm{neg}}=n_-(\mathbf{K}+s\mathbf{I})$. Counting negative pivots is the principle of the Wittrick--Williams algorithm \citep{wittrick1971}, and the detection of limit and bifurcation points from the tangent is standard in nonlinear finite-element analysis \citep{wriggers1990}. The count is exact up to the rounding error of the factorization; it says whether an eigenvalue has changed sign but not how close the nearest eigenvalue is to zero. The lowest eigenvalues are computed in addition by shift-invert Lanczos on the constrained operator, with the shift chosen from the inertia. Shift-invert returns the eigenvalues nearest the shift $\sigma$. When $n_{\mathrm{neg}}=0$ every eigenvalue lies above $-s$, and $\sigma=-10^{-4}$ returns the lowest ones. When $n_{\mathrm{neg}}>0$ a shift below the lowest eigenvalue is located by shift counts ($n_-(\mathbf{K}-\sigma\mathbf{I})=0$), so the eigenvalues nearest $\sigma$ are again the lowest and include every negative one; their number of negative values is checked against $n_{\mathrm{neg}}$. At a limit point an eigenvalue crosses zero together with a sign change of $\mathrm{d}\Lambda/\mathrm{d}s$; at a bifurcation it crosses zero without one \citep{thompson1973}.

\subsection{Validity range}
\label{sec:validity}
A Hookean spring is credible only at moderate element strain. The validity range ends at $\eps^*$, the last step of the initial sequence on which fewer than 5\% of the elements exceed 20\% strain ($P_{20}\le5\%$) and no element exceeds 50\% ($|\eta|_{\max}\le0.5$), or at the end of the stable branch if that comes earlier. The second condition catches localized stretching that a population criterion misses. Because virtual backbone bonds are nearly inextensible, we also report a backbone-aware criterion, $|\eta|_{\mathrm{bb}}\le5\%$, and the backbone-stiffened networks of Section~\ref{sec:robust}. Table~\ref{tab:ver} lists which condition ends each range.

\section{Partner structures, comparators and physical scale}
\label{sec:val}
%======================================================================
\subsection{Partners, mapping and metrics}
Table~\ref{tab:panel} lists the closed or resting structures and their tension-related partners. Two control structures without a tension-related partner enter only the nonlinearity measures: 6CQ8, the K2P channel TREK-1 in complex with the activator ML335 \citep{lolicato2017}, and 6VTK, the acid-sensing ion channel ASIC1a in a desensitized state at low pH \citep{yoder2020}, a proton-gated rather than a classical MS channel. For MscL no open \textit{M.\,tuberculosis} structure exists; the displacement of \textit{M.\,acetivorans} MscL from closed (4Y7K) to expanded (4Y7J) \citep{li2015} is transferred to the aligned C$\alpha$ of 2OAR \citep{steinbacher2007} (sequence identity \V{mscl-seqid}\%). Residues are matched by number and checked by identity for same-species pairs, and by one alignment of the most complete chains for the homologue pair. Chain correspondence is the injective map minimizing the global RMSD, over all permutations for $\le4$ chains and over cyclic and reflected orders otherwise. The primary metric is the C$\alpha$ RMSD over the pre-specified \emph{transmembrane} (TM) subset, the mapped nodes inside the membrane slab, after superposition on that subset; whole-protein RMSDs are reported alongside. The overlap is $|\mathbf{u}\cdot\bm\Delta|/(\|\mathbf{u}\|\|\bm\Delta\|)$ with $\bm\Delta$ the experimental displacement.

\subsection{Comparators}
\label{sec:nulls}
The nonlinear path is represented by its best frame within $\eps\le\eps^*$. It is compared with:
(i) the \emph{best-amplitude linear response}, $s\,\mathbf{u}_L$ with $\mathbf{u}_L=\Kt(\x_0)^{+}\Hm\x_0$ (Eckart-constrained) and $s$ chosen with knowledge of the target, a deliberately generous null;
(ii) the linear response at the \emph{same tension} as the best nonlinear frame;
(iii) the best isotropic in-plane \emph{dilation} of the closed structure about the membrane axis, which contains no network mechanics;
(iv) the best least-squares combination of the $k$ lowest ANM modes ($k=10,30$), an upper bound for linear models built on those modes;
(v) \V{n-rewired} \emph{rewired networks} obtained by degree-preserving double-edge swaps between spatially neighbouring non-backbone elements (new elements shorter than 15\,\AA, \V{rewire-frac}\% of the non-backbone contacts replaced), loaded and analysed exactly as the real network.
The advantage of the nonlinear path is $\Delta_{L-NL}=\mathrm{RMSD}_L-\mathrm{RMSD}_{NL}$, the TM RMSD of the best-amplitude linear response minus that of the best nonlinear frame; it is positive when the nonlinear path is closer to the partner. Differences smaller than $\max(0.2\,\text{\AA},\,3\%)$ are classified as equivalent. By the \emph{approach criterion}, a channel approaches its partner when the TM RMSD falls by more than $\max(0.2\,\text{\AA},5\%)$ of the closed-state value and the overlap exceeds 0.3, so that a change within the equivalence margin is never counted as an approach.

The two best-frame rules are not symmetric, and we keep them as pre-specified. The nonlinear frames are the computed steps, the earliest of which lies at $\eps=$\V{eps-start-min}--\V{eps-start-max}, whereas the amplitude grid of the linear response includes $s=0$, the closed structure. When the closed structure is already the closest frame, the linear comparator returns the closed RMSD and the nonlinear one the RMSD of its earliest step.

\subsection{Physical scale}
\label{sec:phys}
The spring constant is calibrated by matching ANM fluctuations to B-factors: $\langle|\mathbf{u}_i|^2\rangle=k_BT\,(\mathbf{K}^+)_{ii}/\gamma$ and $B_i=8\pi^2\langle|\mathbf{u}_i|^2\rangle/3$, with $\gamma=k_BT\sum_i(\mathbf{K}^+)_{ii}/\sum_i\langle|\mathbf{u}_i|^2\rangle$ and $(\mathbf{K}^+)_{ii}$ the trace of the $3\times3$ diagonal block of the Eckart-constrained pseudo-inverse (exact for $N\le2000$, Hutchinson estimate with 400 probes otherwise). The physical tension is then $\tau_{\mathrm{phys}}=\gamma\,\tau$, where 1\,pN\,\AA$^{-1}$ = 10\,mN\,m$^{-1}$. Cryo-EM B-factors do not measure thermal fluctuations, so we use the range of the three crystal structures for every channel and treat the result as an order-of-magnitude estimate. The physical tension is proportional to the calibrated $\gamma$ and changes with that choice.

The sparse area operator reproduces the shoelace area recomputed directly from the vertex coordinates to $\le$\V{area-err}\ (relative) on every saved frame, and its gradient agrees with central differences to $\le$\V{fd-err}. This check is independent of both solvers, which share $\Hm$.

\subsection{Gauge, cutoff and validity range}
\label{sec:wellposed}
\emph{Gauge.} With only the four exact symmetries constrained, the reaction vanishes to the solver tolerance ($\le$\V{sym-react-max}) for every channel, as the symmetry analysis requires (Fig.~\ref{fig:ver}c). The six Eckart conditions draw a reaction of $q=$\V{rho-min}--\V{rho-max}, the tilting moment, and change the solution by only \V{sym-min}--\V{sym-max}\,\AA{} (tilts of at most \V{tilt-max}$^\circ$) and the validation metric by at most \V{sym-metric-max}\,\AA{} (Fig.~\ref{fig:ver}d). Six isostatic anchors carry the same moment as point reactions of \V{anchreac-min}--\V{anchreac-max} of the load and distort the solution by \V{anch-min}--\V{anch-max}\,\AA{}, shifting the OSCA1.2 validation metric by \V{anch-metric-max}\,\AA. Their constrained tangent is more than an order of magnitude softer than the Eckart one (lowest eigenvalue smaller by a factor of \V{lamratio-anch-min}--\V{lamratio-anch-max} for the anchor sets used here; the factor depends on which six degrees of freedom are anchored): when the stiffness annihilates rigid modes, its Rayleigh quotient on a complement that is not orthogonal to them is smaller, because the rigid components inflate $\|\mathbf{u}\|$ without changing $\mathbf{u}^T\mathbf{K}\mathbf{u}$. The reference-equilibrated load $A'$ raises the Eckart reaction to $q=$\V{eq-rho-min}--\V{eq-rho-max} and increases the anchor distortion in \V{eq-worse} of \V{n-gauge-struct} structures (Fig.~\ref{fig:gauge}). The anchor distortion is thus a consequence of constraining a degree of freedom that the load is not invariant under, not of the discrete residual moment at the reference.

\emph{Cutoff and validity.} At $r_c=8$\,\AA{} the reference networks of Piezo1, TRAAK and OSCA1.2 contain mechanisms (\V{mech-piezo}, \V{mech-traak} and \V{mech-osca} zero eigenvalues of the constrained tangent, counted by the shift count of Section~\ref{sec:inertia}); the 8\,\AA{} networks are therefore not analysed further, and robustness is assessed between 10 and 12\,\AA. The inertia count gives $n_{\mathrm{neg}}=0$ at every accepted step of all paths within $\eps\le\eps^*$ at $r_c=10$ and 12\,\AA{} (\V{n-inertia} factorizations). At $r_c=10$\,\AA{} the validity range ends by localized stretching ($|\eta|_{\max}>0.5$ while $P_{20}<5\%$) in \V{n-strain-end} structures, at the end of the computed range ($\eps\ge0.40$, strain criteria still met) for OSCA1.2, and at the limit point of Section~\ref{sec:snap} for Piezo1 (Table~\ref{tab:ver}, Fig.~\ref{fig:dep}f). The elements that reach $|\eta|_{\max}$ earliest are backbone virtual bonds in \V{n-bb-lead} of six channels.

\section{Snapping}
\label{sec:snap}
\label{sec:piezo}
%======================================================================

Of the eight structures, \V{n-stable} never lose stability under tension at $r_c=10$\,\AA: the inertia count gives $n_{\mathrm{neg}}=0$ to the end of their computed paths, at $\eps=$\V{stable-eps-min}--\V{stable-eps-max}, well beyond their validity ranges (Fig.~\ref{fig:dep}c). Their nonlinear regime is bounded by element strain or by the end of the computed range, not by stability (Fig.~\ref{fig:dep}f). Piezo1 is the exception. Its lowest eigenvalue initially rises, by a factor of \V{piezo-lam-peak}, as the network stiffens globally. The eigenvalue that later reaches zero is not this one: it is the \V{piezo-crit-rank} lowest (\V{piezo-crit-rel}\,$\lambda_1(0)$) until $\Delta\eps\approx$\V{piezo-crit-deps} before the fold, then descends through the two lowest and becomes $\lambda_1$, at \V{piezo-lam-l1}\,$\lambda_1(0)$ on the last stable step (Fig.~\ref{fig:dep}c, Fig.~\ref{fig:limit}c, Table~\ref{tab:ver}).

Load-controlled Newton stops at $\eps=$\V{piezo-lc-eps}. Arclength continuation shows why (Fig.~\ref{fig:limit}b): $\mathrm{d}\Lambda/\mathrm{d}s$ changes sign at $\tau_{c}=$\V{piezo-tauc} ($\eps=$\V{piezo-epsc}), and exactly there the inertia count changes from 0 to 1 negative eigenvalue. For the discretized load this is a limit point, not a bifurcation, and the end of the stable branch under tension control. Beyond it the continuation descends along an unstable branch to a second fold at $\tau=$\V{piezo-tauc2} ($\eps=$\V{piezo-epsc2}, the value of the continuation point itself) and continues through further folds, the signature of successive local snaps.

The event is localized in \V{piezo-snap-elems}. Approaching the fold, the largest strain rises from \V{piezo-maxe-pre} to \V{piezo-maxe-post} within $\Delta\eps\approx0.004$. Only one of the three segments snaps. The reference structure is trimeric to $\V{piezo-c3-rmsd}$\,\AA, but its threefold axis is tilted by \V{piezo-c3-tilt}$^\circ$ from the membrane normal of the oriented structure (a rotation by $120^\circ$ about the normal leaves a C$\alpha$ RMSD of \V{piezo-c3-zrot}\,\AA), so the 4\,\AA{} layers, the outer shells and hence the load are not exactly threefold symmetric. This discrete asymmetry, not round-off, selects the segment: approaching the fold \V{piezo-n-desc} constrained eigenvalue descends while the other low eigenvalues stay nearly constant. For an exactly symmetric load the critical point would involve the three equivalent segments and could be a symmetry-breaking bifurcation, and the limit point reported here would be its imperfect counterpart. Whether it is a limit point or a bifurcation of the symmetric problem, and how $\tau_c$ depends on the tilt, is open; a group-theoretic analysis of the critical eigenspace \citep{healey1988} and an imperfection-sensitivity analysis \citep{ikeda2010} would settle it.

Explicit VFIFE started at rest from the limit point at $\tau=(1+10^{-3})\tau_c$ comes to rest after \V{snap-steps} explicit steps at an equilibrium that \V{snap-cmp}. Under increasing tension the primary branch therefore ends in a local snap-through of a short segment, not in a global transition. Global stiffening and local softening coexist on the same path: the segment that snaps carries the softening, while the rest of the network continues to stiffen. Past the fold the lowest eigenvalue is negative on \V{piezo-n-lamneg} of the \V{piezo-n-unstable} continuation points with $n_{\mathrm{neg}}=1$ and reaches \V{piezo-lam-post}\,$\lambda_1(0)$ ten points past L$_1$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:limit}c).

The limit point is established for the reference network only, and lies at high element strain. By definition $\eps^*$ cannot extend beyond the end of the stable branch, so for Piezo1 the validity range ends at the limit point; this is a consequence of the definition, not evidence that the limit point marks the end of the Hookean regime. The strain criteria are still met there, but the largest strain, \V{piezo-maxe-pre}--\V{piezo-maxe-post} near the fold, is far beyond the backbone criterion, which ends the range at $\eps=$\V{piezo-bb-epsstar}. At $r_c=12$\,\AA{} load control stopped at $\eps=$\V{piezo12-stall}, beyond the validity range ($\eps^*=$\V{piezo12-epsstar}), with $n_{\mathrm{neg}}=0$ at every accepted step and without a negative eigenvalue at the last one; we have not continued that path. With the backbone stiffened 10 and 100 times the inertia count also stays at $n_{\mathrm{neg}}=0$, up to $\eps=$\V{piezo-kbb10-end} and \V{piezo-kbb100-end}, where load control stopped; in the 10-fold case $\lambda_1$ had fallen to \V{piezo-kbb10-lamend}\,$\lambda_1(0)$, and that path was not continued either. Whether these networks reach a limit point beyond their computed range is therefore not known.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:disc}
%======================================================================
\paragraph{How the nonlinear response differs from the linearization} For an elastic network compared with experiment through a displacement, the three departures have different weights. Stiffening is universal and large but does not affect the shape, because the amplitude is chosen; it explains why the usual linearization error $E_{NL}$ overstates the importance of nonlinearity for the shape. Stress stiffening of soft, nearly floppy modes is familiar from under-constrained fibre and random networks \citep{wyart2008,broedersz2014}; whether that mechanism, rather than the rotation of stiff elements, dominates here has not been separated. Turning is what a nonlinear calculation can add to the shape, and its rank across structures is anticipated by $\chi$ before any path is computed. Turning is required for a nonlinear advantage but does not guarantee one: in Piezo1 it points toward the partner, while MscL, with the second-largest $\chi$, is ``NL worse''. The loss of stability does not improve the agreement; it bounds the regime in which a smooth path exists.

\paragraph{What the nonlinear analysis gives that the linearization does not} It gives a verified equilibrium path with a stability check by inertia count at every step and an explicit validity range; the turning, which the linearization cannot represent; and, for Piezo1 at the reference cutoff, a localized limit point. For the five channels other than Piezo1 it adds these, not accuracy against the partner structures.

\paragraph{Limitations} The Piezo1 gain over the rescaled linearization equals that of an isotropic in-plane dilation (\V{piezo-nl} vs \V{piezo-dil}\,\AA) and appears also in rewired networks, so it is not evidence for the specific contacts of the network. It decreases with the cutoff (\V{piezo-delta}\,\AA{} at 10\,\AA, \V{piezo-rc12-delta}\,\AA{} at 12\,\AA). The limit point was found at $r_c=10$\,\AA{} only, at element strains well beyond the backbone criterion, and its nature in the exactly symmetric problem is open. The network topology is fixed: contacts are neither broken nor formed along the path. The tensions that the MscS and MscL best frames require are \V{lysis-x-min}--\V{lysis-x-max} times a typical lytic tension (below). Springs are uniform and Hookean except in the backbone variants, and material nonlinearity and sequence-dependent stiffness are absent. The membrane is represented by a flat polygon area without curvature, thickness or lipid-specific interactions. Two partner structures are imperfect references: the MscL target is a homologue displacement, and the MscS partner is a mutant crystal structure. Both nonlinear and linear responses are represented by their best frame or amplitude, so the comparison measures how close each \emph{can} come, not a blind prediction, and the two best-frame rules differ at the closed state (Section~\ref{sec:nulls}). The post-limit-point response of Piezo1 consists of several folds that we have not followed to completion. The validity thresholds, the equivalence margin and the approach criterion were set during method development; we report their sensitivity so that readers can apply their own. The tension calibration rests on crystallographic B-factors of three channels and is an order-of-magnitude estimate. The model is quasi-static: the explicit time integration used for the snap is a solution device with fictitious masses, not a physical time history.

\paragraph{Tension and shape} Stiffening fixes how much tension a given shape costs, and here the uniform network is too stiff. Calibrated against crystallographic B-factors ($\gamma=$\V{gamma-min}--\V{gamma-max}\,pN\,\AA$^{-1}$, Table~\ref{tab:phys}), the best frames of MscS and MscL require \V{mscs-tphys-best} and \V{mscl-tphys-best}\,mN\,m$^{-1}$, \V{lysis-x-min}--\V{lysis-x-max} times typical lytic tensions of about \V{lysis}\,mN\,m$^{-1}$ \citep{evans2003}. With the same calibration the Piezo1 best frame lies at \V{piezo-tphys-best}\,mN\,m$^{-1}$ and its limit point at \V{piezo-tphys-c}\,mN\,m$^{-1}$, comparable with lysis and above the tensions at which Piezo1 opens in cells \citep{lewis2015,cox2016}, because its large, weakly connected blades give it the largest compliance of the panel ($\eps_\tau=$\V{piezo-ept} against \V{ept-min}--\V{ept-max} for the five other channels, OSCA1.2 excepted). This comparison depends on the calibration: with Piezo1's own cryo-EM value, $\gamma=$\V{piezo-gamma-own}\,pN\,\AA$^{-1}$, the limit point lies at \V{piezo-tphys-c-own}\,mN\,m$^{-1}$. The model therefore predicts shapes, not tensions.

\paragraph{Relation to ANM and to continuum models} The comparison is between a network and its own linearization, with the same springs, cutoff and load, so it isolates geometric nonlinearity. Linear response and perturbation-response approaches \citep{ikeguchi2005,atilgan2009} share the linearization examined here; the adaptive ANM \citep{yang2009aanm} updates the reference but needs the end state, whereas the tension path needs only the load. A comparison with these schemes, and with a second-order perturbation response along the whole path, is left for future work. Continuum, finite-element and coarse-grained gating models \citep{wiggins2004,tang2006,bathe2008,yefimov2008,louhivuori2010} resolve the membrane mechanics that the polygon load omits but prescribe or coarse-grain the channel geometry differently. The present framework is complementary: it keeps the structure-derived network and replaces the membrane by its tension.

\paragraph{Observations for related network problems} Three observations may be useful for soft, locally under-constrained networks under configuration-dependent conservative loads. (i)~The gauge should follow the symmetries of the load, not of the stored energy. A projected-area load is not invariant under tilt, and constraining tilt draws a reaction, which nodal anchors concentrate and amplify. (ii)~Splitting the linearization error into amplitude and direction separates the part that a rescaled linear response cannot reproduce; here a second-order indicator ranked that part. (iii)~Global stiffening can coexist with a local limit point, whose critical eigenvalue need not be the lowest one along most of the path, and a population strain criterion should be complemented by a maximum-strain criterion, because the response localizes.
